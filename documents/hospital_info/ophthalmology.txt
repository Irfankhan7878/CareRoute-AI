1. Department Description & Services
The Eye Institute provides comprehensive visual assessments, refraction testing, screening and treatment for glaucoma, cataracts, diabetic retinopathy, dry eye disease, corneal disorders, and ocular surface inflammatory conditions.

2. Registration & Intake Instructions
Check in at Building D Entrance Reception. Initial visual acuity screening using Snellen charts and intraocular pressure tonometry will be performed by certified ophthalmic technicians before physician examination.

3. Required Documents
- Valid identification card and medical insurance policy card.
- Current eyeglasses (distance and reading prescriptions) and contact lens prescription details.
- Records of previous ocular surgeries (e.g., LASIK, cataract lens implant cards, retinal laser records).
- All current ophthalmic eye drops (e.g., glaucoma drops, artificial tears).

4. Preparation Instructions
- IMPORTANT: Diagnostic pupil dilation drops may be administered during your visit. Dilation causes temporary light sensitivity and blurred near vision for 4 to 6 hours.
- Arrange for a companion or public transportation to assist you home; DO NOT drive yourself after pupil dilation.
- Bring a pair of dark sunglasses to minimize discomfort from sunlight when leaving the eye clinic.
- Remove contact lenses prior to the examination if evaluating dry eye or ocular surface irritation.

5. Department-Specific Intake Notes
Sudden, painless loss of vision in one eye, flashes of light accompanied by a curtain falling across vision (suspected retinal detachment), or acute severe eye pain with rainbow halos requires immediate emergency triage.
